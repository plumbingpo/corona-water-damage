Water Heater Leak Cleanup Help in Corona

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. Until someone closes the cold water valve, it keeps running. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

In Corona, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. Garage leaks wet whatever is stored on the floor, along with the wall the garage shares with the house. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut the Power or GasElectric units get shut down at the breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Fabric and cardboard absorb water fast, then keep it pressed to the concrete and walls.
The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Drain valves that crack, work loose or stop sealing completely.
  • Sediment and corrosion that eat through the bottom of an aging steel tank.
  • A blocked or rusted drain pan under an attic or closet heater that overflows instead of draining.
  • Connections that were not fully tightened during a repair or a new installation.
  • Excess pressure in the tank from a failed thermostat or a missing expansion tank.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Closet Walls

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. Swollen baseboards and a wall bottom that is still damp within are typical.

Garage Wall Base

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Items Stored Near the Heater

Boxes, furniture and keepsakes stored near a garage or closet heater sit in the water first. Mold can develop on damp contents that are not promptly dried or tossed, while cardboard boxes disintegrate.

Platform and Subfloor

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. It can rot and weaken, which matters when a new full tank is set on it.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

Our Process

How We Handle Water Heater Leak Cleanup

1

Extract the Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Inspect the Surrounding Area

To learn the full reach of the water, our crew meters and scans the platform, the bottoms of the walls, neighboring rooms and the ceiling below if there is one.

3

Remove Wet Material

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. In attics we clear wet insulation so the ceiling joists and drywall below can be dried.

4

Dry and Monitor

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. Our crew logs the readings and lets you know as soon as the area is ready for a replacement heater.
